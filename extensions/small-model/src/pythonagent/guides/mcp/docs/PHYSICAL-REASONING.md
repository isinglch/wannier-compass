# Physical interpretation of projected bands

The decision order, required evidence, and criterion priority are defined in [WORKFLOW-DECISIONS.md](WORKFLOW-DECISIONS.md). This document explains physical distinctions and adds no separate selection procedure.

## Orbitals and projections

Chemical main inspection candidates are s for the s block, p for the p block, d for the d block, and available f for the f series. An f-series hint does not establish f occupation or task relevance, including at the chemically variable ends of the series. Inspect additional shells and supported nominally empty d shells, including optional Ca/Sr/Ba d hints, against the independently declared task. Group 13 does not mandate an s+p basis. Selected shells can occur in valence, antibonding conduction, and mixed regions; a fixed interval near EF or a single peak cannot establish their task role. Use unnormalized raw element-by-angular-momentum projections and compare channels on the same `(k,n)` state.

Resolved f channels follow this same decision order: declare the physical task, review raw f weights against other shells on each state, distinguish task/empty/deep/background roles, choose independent DIS and frozen edges, and apply the frozen counterexample veto only to protection. Complete f projections contain seven spatial orbitals per atom, doubled in the current both-spin spinor convention. Evidence-format support does not validate the underlying DFT treatment of a correlated f system. Execution remains within the documented semilocal Hamiltonian and spin-mode contract; DFT+U and dual collinear spectra require separate support and must not be silently relabeled.

PROCAR atomic-sphere projections do not cover all space, and s/p/d/f labels do not identify the radial principal quantum number. Weak sphere weight can represent interstitial character. A strong deep peak of the same angular channel does not automatically belong to the current valence model. Omitting a chemical starting shell requires its role across the complete target and relevant conduction states, rather than only relative weakness. Wyckoff/site-resolved few-band models have a different scope from this element-by-shell complete-manifold workflow.

Residual weight outside a deep block can be secondary hybridization; nonzero weight, a strong isolated peak, or a narrow energy spread alone does not require keeping that angular channel. Conversely, apparent secondary character does not prove that a reduced basis can reproduce the requested states. Treat omission as a reviewed model hypothesis, retain mixing uncertainty, and validate the same predeclared target after fitting.

For a chosen reduced basis, a hybridized task state can fail the frozen-only screen because an omitted channel outweighs the selected projection. That result limits forced protection under the current criterion; it does not establish that the omitted channel needs its own Wannier orbital. Keep the basis hypothesis distinct, review the inner boundary, and test the task state through disentanglement outside frozen. Adding a channel only to clear this screen would transfer a window veto into a basis rule. When a reduced-basis comparison needs a different frozen boundary, compare the larger model at that same boundary as a control and retain the original evaluation range.

A basis is an effective representation, not a request to fit every atomic bonding state. A task-relevant p/d model can retain mixed DFT states in DIS while omitting a deep concentrated s block and its explicit s channel. Lack of a global gap does not change that option. If an extra s channel is useful for necessary low conduction, review its task role separately from whether deep bonding bands belong to the target. Neither adding s nor dropping s automatically determines the lower window.

## Valence

Deep occupied non-target blocks are often identifiable, but filled shells can still participate in bonding. The global gap is `min_k E_(n+1) − max_k E_n`; the minimum direct gap is `min_k [E_(n+1)(k) − E_n(k)]`. A positive direct gap and negative global gap permit separation at each sampled k but provide no common energy cut. Hybridization and target/background role exchange can complicate either case.

Wannier construction remains possible. Start `dis_min` below the lowest target weight, allowing some background for disentanglement; independently raise `frozen_min` past unwanted/weakly projected lower states. If a scalar boundary cannot separate overlapping energies, protect the clean target interior, retain mixed states in outer, and inspect their fit. A global gap alone does not prove unchanged projection roles.

`exclude_bands` is optional and uses k-independent original DFT indices. Restrict it to a low-energy occupied prefix with non-target character and sampled global-gap evidence. A direct gap cannot substitute for the global exclusion gap. Continue without exclusion if no prefix qualifies. Even with a gap, `dis_min=frozen_min` is only an explicitly reviewed simplification. One angular channel can contribute to deep and target blocks; excluding a deep block does not delete that channel from the basis.

## Conduction and freezing

Empty states can be diffuse and can contain character outside atomic projections. `dis_max` must supply target states and sufficient disentanglement space while accounting for the incomplete computed-band edge. Approximately 70% cumulative conduction weight and tail onset are initial diagnostics whose denominator is the reviewed reference's conduction part. Strong deep-valence weight cannot determine the conduction edge. Mark a reference touching the last computed band as right-censored; absence of an observed tail alone does not establish a need for more NBANDS.

`frozen_max` protects clean target states and need not equal the highest occupied energy. EF `+0.5 eV` for metals/semimetals or CBM `+0.5 eV` for insulators is a starting candidate. A path gap alone does not establish insulation throughout the BZ. For each added interval, review target membership, selected-channel dominance, background branches, and role exchange. Only afterward apply the negative screen: lower-edge problems review `frozen_min`, upper-edge problems review `frozen_max`. No counterexample creates expansion evidence or changes basis/outer.

Compare fits on the same predeclared physical target; narrowing frozen or the scoring region does not establish improvement. Window-only changes can reuse identity-matched matrices; changing basis or exclusions requires new matching matrices. Check actual-mesh execution gates after interface generation. Real Wannier runs use `num_iter=0`; disentanglement iterations and maximal-localization iterations are distinct controls.
